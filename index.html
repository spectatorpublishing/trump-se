One Year Later:
Columbia’s Deal with the Trump Administration

Letter From the Editor

Dear readers,

After months of escalating negotiations and years of on-campus antisemitism allegations, Columbia announced a $221 million settlement with President Donald Trump’s administration on July 23, 2025. The agreement, which many in the University community viewed as a concession, resumed the flow of federal funding that the government cut four months prior and settled investigations brought by the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission into Columbia’s alleged civil rights violations.

Throughout the settlement talks, Spectator’s reporting has charted the complex series of interactions between the federal government and Columbia resulting in the loss and restoration of $400 million in federal funding, threats to the school’s accreditation, and an increasingly tangled web of policy changes. These changes saw the University agree to measures regarding transparency in its hiring and admissions data as well as the adoption of a controversial antisemitism definition that some critics say chills free speech.

In this special edition, Spectator has compiled articles spanning three University presidents, charting how the settlement has impacted student discipline, Immigration and Customs Enforcement policies, and presidential turnover at Columbia. As the University seeks stable leadership, we hope this edition serves as a record of the recent turmoil and an illumination of what is to come.

Sincerely,
